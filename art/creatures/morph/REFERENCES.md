# Morph references (UI overhaul phase 7, A1)

What each reference taught, in our own words. No frames are copied or stored; these are notes from having watched them, not from a new study of stills.
Written with Klaus's art (A2), after his first poses were already blocked, so it records what those poses lean on and what the next three bosses should take.

## Film: the cut hidden by the pose

**An American Werewolf in London (1981).** The change is shown in a lit room with nowhere to hide, so it is sold by the body, not by darkness: a hand
stretches, the back arches up, and the man ends on all fours before the head has finished changing. Taken from it:
* the **stretch**: the body gets bigger while it moves, never as a size change on a still pose (our wolf's root grows from 0.75 to 1 only while it tips forward);
* the **hunch as the meeting point**: a man bent over and a wolf reared up have nearly the same outline, which is where our cut sits (tick 19 to 20);
* the change **ends on the ground**: the last beat is weight arriving on the forelegs.

**The Howling (1981).** The change happens standing, facing the camera, and takes its time: the figure swells in place before anything else moves. Taken
from it: the wolf's first frame is **upright**, and the first ticks are small (a breath, a turn of the head) before the drop. What we do not take: its length.
We have one second.

## This universe

**Teen Wolf.** A shift is fast and readable from far away: the **head goes back**, the shoulders come forward, the hands open into claws, and the sound
(a roar) lands with the pose. The full wolves arrive by movement (a leap, a landing), not by a slow change. Taken: the man's head snaps back at the first
bone crack (tick 7), the claws come up one after the other, and the wolf's half is a landing.

**The Originals / The Vampire Diaries.** A werewolf's change is **bones breaking one at a time**, each with its own sound, the body jerking with each, then
folding forward onto its hands. Klaus, a hybrid, does it at will and fast. Taken: the man's half is two hits, not one glide (keys at ticks 12 and 16, on the
two crack cues at 7 and 14), with the right side first and the left following; Klaus's rhythm has a snap and a recoil, then a held breath.

## Real wolves and dogs

* **Coming down from a rear or a jump:** the forepaws do not land together. One leads, the other follows within a stride; the shoulders sink between the
  legs to take the weight, the head dips a beat after the chest, and the rump and tail arrive last. Ours: left paw at 0.60 s (the LAND cue, tick 32), right at
  0.65 s, chest lowest at 0.68 s, head lowest at 0.72 to 0.74 s, tail down at 0.80 to 0.85 s.
* **A sitting or rearing canid keeps its tail on the ground behind it**, lifted from the base; it does not point down through the floor.
* **Rising:** a dog pushes off its forelegs after rocking its weight back onto the haunches (a bow first). Ours: `shift_out` bows for 0.2 s, then shoves off
  the right forepaw, then the left.
* **Ears and fur follow the body late:** ears flatten at an impact and come forward after; the ruff and hackles stand up at the hit and settle slower.

## A game that does it well

**The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, Beast Form.** Third person, in the world, no cut to a cinematic: the man hunches, the body swells, and it ends with the beast's
own roar animation. It reads at gameplay distance because the outline changes first and the detail second. Taken: silhouette first. Not taken: it locks the
player for several seconds; ours is 2 s and the boss stays hittable.

## Poses to hit, per boss

Eight stills per boss (man peak, wolf first frame, landing, settle and the ones between), as frame numbers: `previews/<boss>/POSES.md`, written for all
four. What each takes from the notes above: Klaus the snap and the fall; Deucalion the standing change and one heavy hit (he stays upright, so his
landing is a hand); Peter the dog that sets itself down, one paw then the other; the Beast the swelling in place, then weight arriving all at once.
